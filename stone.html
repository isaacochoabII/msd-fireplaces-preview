Helping you choose the right stone for your project

See the colour in your own light

Stone can look very different in person than it does in a photo. We provide samples you can take home to see how the colour, texture and variation work with the surrounding finishes and lighting in the room or exterior area where it will be installed.

The profile should suit the space

Larger stone can overwhelm a smaller area, while the detail and definition of a smaller profile may be lost when viewed from the road. We’ll help you choose a size and style that works with your home, the area being covered and the distance from which it will be viewed.

Installation changes the finished look

Stone installed tightly together for a dry-stack look will have a very different appearance than stone installed with visible mortar joints. The colour and width of the mortar can also change the overall look. We’ll help you compare the options and account for openings, columns, returns and other finishing details when planning and quoting your project.

How it goes on

Mortar-set or screw-on?

Stone can be installed one piece at a time with mortar or in panels that screw onto the wall. We carry options for both methods and can help you choose the right one for your project. Both can be installed year-round, although mortar-set installations need to be enclosed and heated during cold weather.

Mortar-set

Mortar-set — the traditional method

Each piece is installed individually with mortar over a properly prepared surface.

  • Available in natural and manufactured stone
  • Can have visible mortar joints or a tighter dry-stack look
  • Pieces can be cut and fitted around arches, columns, openings and other details
  • Allows the installer to adjust the layout piece by piece

A good option for: Traditional installation and projects with custom details

Screw-on

Screw-on — a DIY-friendly option

The stone comes in panels that screw onto the wall, much like siding.

  • Straightforward for contractors and DIYers
  • No mortar joints to finish
  • Designed to create the look of individually laid stone
  • Available in several colours and stone styles

A good option for: DIY projects and straightforward installations

Stone questions, answered

Natural stone veneer is quarried stone cut thin. Manufactured stone veneer is cast from moulds taken off real stone and coloured through the mix. Natural gives you genuine variation and weight; manufactured is lighter, easier on a budget, faster to install, and comes in far more colours. We carry both, and the honest answer for most projects is that once it's on the wall, very few people can tell — so it usually comes down to budget and the look you're after.

Mortar-set is the traditional method: each piece is installed individually with mortar over a properly prepared surface, which lets the installer cut and fit around arches, columns and openings and adjust the layout as they go. Screw-on comes as panels that fasten straight to the wall, much like siding — no mortar joints to finish, and straightforward enough for contractors and DIYers alike. We carry options for both methods and can help you choose the right one for your project.

Yes — both methods can be installed year-round. Mortar-set installations need to be enclosed and heated during cold weather, which adds to the setup on a winter job. Screw-on panels have no mortar to cure, so they are the simpler option once the temperature drops. Talk to us about timing and we will tell you what your project needs.

Yes, when it's installed properly. The stone itself is freeze-thaw rated; what fails is almost always the wall behind it — missing drainage plane, no weep screed, or mortar work done in the cold. That's why we're picky about who we recommend for installation and why we'll walk you through the assembly before you buy.

It depends on the brand, the profile and how much corner you have, so we quote per project rather than quoting a number that turns out to be wrong. Bring us your square footage and your linear feet of outside corners and we'll give you a real number the same way we'd quote it for a contractor.

Corner pieces are moulded or cut differently and cost more per piece than flats, so they're sold by the linear foot rather than the square foot. A house with a lot of returns, columns and window surrounds can carry a surprising amount of corner.
